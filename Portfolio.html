Projects

01

Pipe Elbow Modelling & Simulation

The problem: Client needed a comparative ANSYS study of 90° pipe bends to identify the optimal L/R ratio. With upstream conditions fixed, the task was to assess how bend tightness affects turbulent kinetic energy, pressure drop and velocity, and what that means for frictional losses, erosion and pump duty.

My approach: Ran iterative simulations, refining each configuration and documenting every change and result to keep the process traceable and repeatable.

What I learned: The value of rigorous documentation, technical report writing for a client audience, and the fundamentals of governing fluid flow models.

02

Hydrogen Liquefaction Process Design

The problem: Design a hydrogen liquefaction process in Aspen HYSYS meeting fixed constraints on product temperature, pressure, heat exchanger approach temperatures and energy use, then report on performance and cost.

My approach: Justified each design choice against thermodynamic principles and peer-reviewed literature, using a two-loop refrigeration cycle (precooling and subcooling with different refrigerant compositions) to optimise heat exchange.

What I learned: How to research a problem thoroughly enough to defend assumptions and design choices, and a working command of HYSYS for process design.

03

Biodiesel Plant Design & Feasibility Study

The problem: Group project to design a biodiesel facility using Western Australia's canola supply, covering reaction pathway selection, plant capacity, and a feasibility study benchmarked against comparable plants.

My approach: Delegated work to each member's strengths, tracked individual responsibilities, and kept the group on a strict timeline.

What I learned: How to run a feasibility study using CAPEX/OPEX estimates, and the teamwork needed to delegate, stay consistent, and communicate across a group.

04

Distillation Column Design

The problem: Scale up a lab-scale sieve-tray column's ethanol-water separation to industrial size, comparing McCabe-Thiele graphical analysis against a HYSYS simulation to explain the differences.

My approach: Modelled the column both ideally (McCabe-Thiele) and via HYSYS, traced the real shortfall to weeping and entrainment, then used the closely-matching HYSYS model to set revised tray dimensions and duty.

What I learned: How to back scale-up recommendations with solid evidence and present technical comparisons clearly to a non-specialist audience.
